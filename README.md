# 🌑 Outpost 7

Open `index.html` in any browser (no install needed). Six lunar outposts were lost within their first week. You command the seventh: **survive 30 days (one full lunar cycle), stay under the radiation limit and bring home rock samples**. It is a choice-based survival game with no 2D or 3D world: every day you decide how to spend your hours, and every day brings problems.

- **Astronaut training academy (real NASA training):** before the mission a 10-lesson course teaches how real astronauts train: selection and requirements, underwater spacewalk practice in the Neutral Buoyancy Laboratory, T-38 flying and survival training, reduced-gravity flights and g-forces, staying healthy in space (exercise, bone loss, the Twins Study), space radiation and dose limits, ISS life support systems, emergency simulations (Apollo 13, Apollo 11's 1202 alarm, the 2015 ammonia alarm, CAPCOM), teamwork, language and robotics, and Moon geology training. Each lesson first teaches, then quizzes. Graduating earns bonus Science Points.
- **How to play (separate):** a short 'How to play Outpost 7' page follows training, and the 📖 How to play button (always at the top) opens the full 12-section game manual. There is no landing: you start straight on Day 1.
- **Live telemetry and live space weather:** the top of the 📈 Data tab has real-time squiggly sensor traces (radiation dose rate, power bus, cabin oxygen pressure, habitat and outside temperature, crew heart rate) that update four times a second and react to your base (the dose rate spikes in a radiation storm). Below it is a live NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center feed: the real solar X-ray flux from the GOES satellite for the last 6 hours (A, B, C, M and X flare classes) and the planetary Kp index, refreshed every minute. If the real Sun has had a medium or large flare, radiation events become more likely in your game. The NOAA feed needs network access, so it works when you open `index.html` directly in a browser; some hosted previews block it and show a note instead.
- **Data charts:** the 📈 Data tab shows how much of everything you have: bars for the seven meters, materials in stock, the day's power budget, modules by type and shielding, plus line charts of oxygen/water/food, power/health/morale, radiation against the sickness and limit lines, and Science Points. Hover or tap for exact values, and every chart has a table view. Chart colors use a validated colorblind-safe palette.
- **Dramatic opening:** pressing New Outpost opens a black page where the message from Space Mission, the company, types out letter by letter in Audiowide: you are the only hope, the company has failed infinite times, the budget is low, this is the last chance. Tap Skip to read it instantly.
- **Counters:** Oxygen, Water, Food and Science Points each have their own big counter at the top of the screen (with how much each drains per day and how many Science Points you earned today). The Data tab has separate charts for oxygen, water and food.
- **Science tasks:** 55 tasks in five levels: Very easy (1 hour, +5 SP), Easy (2 hours, +10 or +15), Medium (5 hours, +20), Hard (7 hours, +25) and Very hard (10 hours, +40). Harder levels unlock on days 3, 5 and 8.
- **Choices all day:** each day, spend limited hours on Maintain jobs, Science tasks, Explore trips, building or Mission Control quizzes.
- **Build anything you type:** type what you want to build into the Build tab (for example "greenhouse", "oxygen" or "dust") and pick from 105 modules (or browse by category). Choose a structure material (aluminum, titanium, steel, honeycomb, carbon fiber or regolith bricks), which changes time, power and units needed and gives strength, radiation shield and insulation. Building costs **hours and power** per hour of work. Some modules have a set material and set time (the Kilopower reactor needs titanium and 8 hours, radiation shelters need regolith). Cargo landers deliver materials **every day**, with a big load every 3rd day. You can also mine regolith, request material packs, and build mining and manufacturing modules.
- **Major problems happen often:** a morning problem (45% to 85% chance by mode) and a night event every day, each a choice with real trade-offs. 32 events across radiation, technical failures, medical emergencies, environment, supply delays and crew morale, plus EVA incidents on trips outside.
- **Radiation:** about 1.4 mSv a day builds up on the surface, solar particle events and cosmic ray surges add more, and every trip outside adds dose. Shelters, regolith burial, thick materials, flare alarms and monitors cut it. Radiation sickness starts at 120 mSv and 250 mSv ends the mission. NASA's career limit is 600 mSv.
- **Actual NASA data:** the lunar day (about 29.5 Earth days, shown as 15 sunlit and 15 dark days), temperatures (about 127 °C to -173 °C and below -246 °C in shadowed craters), 1.62 m/s² gravity, 1.3 second radio delay, oxygen needs, ISS water recycling, Apollo and LCROSS sample facts, and a different real NASA fact each day. Events and modules cite real missions and tech (Apollo 11's 1202 alarm, Apollo 13's CO2 filter, the ISS suit leak, Mir's fire, Kilopower, MOXIE, the electrodynamic dust shield, Artemis).
- **Explore:** trips to 8 rock sample sites, the shadowed ice crater, a lava tube and the wrecks of Outposts 1 to 6, costing hours, oxygen and dose.
- **The Six Lost Outposts:** each failed crew has a named story with crew, logs and a real NASA lesson. Find the wrecks to read them in the Archive.
- **Win:** survive all 30 days, collect the required rock samples (4 to 6 by mode) and load them into the return capsule. A finish-mission sequence plays.
- Modes: Cadet, Junior, Commander, Veteran and Free Play (no failure).
